# How the Internet Is Built — Practice

### P1. Backbone medium

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Type:** MCQ · **Concepts:** submarine cables

Most data between India and Europe travels over:

- A) Geostationary satellites
- B) Submarine optical fibre
- C) Microwave towers
- D) Copper telephone lines

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) Submarine optical fibre

**Explanation:** Fibre offers far higher capacity and lower latency than satellites; satellites serve mainly remote areas.

</details>

### P2. Satellite latency

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Type:** Calculation · **Concepts:** propagation delay

A geostationary satellite orbits about 36,000 km above the equator. Radio travels at about 300,000 km/s. Estimate the one-way delay from a ground station, up to the satellite and down to another ground station.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Distance ≈ 2 × 36,000 = 72,000 km. Delay ≈ 72,000 ÷ 300,000 = **0.24 s (240 ms)** one way — about 480 ms for a request and its response, before any processing.

</details>

### P3. Transit or peering?

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Type:** Scenario · **Concepts:** IXP, peering

Two Chennai ISPs exchange traffic between their customers at a local IXP without payment. Another small ISP pays a large carrier to reach the rest of the world. Name each arrangement.

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The first is peering (at an IXP); the second is transit.

</details>
