# Basic Charts — Practice

### P1. Monthly trend

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Type:** Chart · **Concepts:** line chart

You want to display monthly sales trends. Which chart is most appropriate?

- A) Pie
- B) Line
- C) Scatter
- D) None

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) Line

Lines connect values in time order and show the trend. A pie shows shares of a whole, not change over time.

</details>

### P2. Share of the total

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Type:** Chart · **Concepts:** pie chart

You want to show what percentage of total sales each of three products contributes. Which chart fits best?

- A) Line
- B) Pie
- C) Bar with dates
- D) Line with markers

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) Pie

It is a part-of-a-whole question with one series and few categories.

</details>

### P3. Long names

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Type:** Chart · **Concepts:** bar chart

You compare total expenses across 12 departments with long names such as "Research and Development". Which chart is most readable?

- A) Pie
- B) Line
- C) Bar
- D) Column with rotated labels

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** C) Bar

Horizontal bars give long category names room to read. A pie with 12 slices is hard to compare, and a line implies an order such as time.

</details>

### P4. The odd bar

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Type:** Chart · **Concepts:** selecting data

A column chart of product sales shows Laptop, Mobile and a third, very tall bar named "Total". What went wrong, and how do you fix it?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The selection included the total row, so Excel plotted the total as another category. Select only the product rows (labels and values with the header) — or use Chart Design → Select Data and remove the Total row.

</details>
