# Blood Relations

## Concept

Blood-relation questions describe family links in words ("A is the brother of B's mother") and ask how two people are related. The reliable method is to **draw a small family tree** as you read, rather than holding the chain in your head.

Two habits prevent almost every mistake:

1. **Track generations.** Parents are one level up, children one level down, siblings and spouses on the same level.
2. **Track gender only from the question.** "Brother", "mother", "he", "she" tell you gender; **names do not**. If gender is not stated, the answer may have to be "brother or sister" or "cannot be determined".

## Key Terms

| Relation | Meaning |
|----------|---------|
| Paternal | On the father's side (paternal uncle = father's brother) |
| Maternal | On the mother's side (maternal uncle = mother's brother) |
| Uncle / aunt | Parent's brother / sister (also the spouse of a parent's sibling) |
| Nephew / niece | Sibling's son / daughter |
| Cousin | Child of an uncle or aunt |
| Father-in-law / mother-in-law | Spouse's father / mother |
| Brother-in-law | Spouse's brother, or sister's husband |
| Sister-in-law | Spouse's sister, or brother's wife |
| Grandson / granddaughter | Child's son / daughter |
| Only son / only daughter | The one son / one daughter (other children of the other gender may exist) |

## Rules

### Drawing the family tree

```text
Notation
  A(+)       male          A(−)       female
  A = B      married couple
  A — B      siblings
  │          parent above, child below

Example: "B is C's mother; A is B's brother"

      A(+) — B(−)
             │
             C
```

From the diagram, A is the brother of C's mother → **A is C's maternal uncle**.

### Reading chains

Start from the **last** person named and work back, or start from the person whose relation is asked. "A is the father of B's mother" → B's mother is one level above B; her father is two levels above → A is B's maternal grandfather.

### "Pointing to a photo" statements

These describe the person in the photo through the speaker's family. Replace the phrase step by step with simpler relations:

"She is the daughter of my grandfather's only son."
→ my grandfather's only son = my father
→ the daughter of my father = my sister (or me, if the speaker is female — read the question for gender)

### Coded relations

Symbols stand for relations (e.g. P + Q means "P is the mother of Q"). Translate each symbol into words, link the people in order, then draw the tree.

## Solving Approach

1. Read the whole statement once; note every stated gender.
2. Draw the tree one relation at a time, placing generations vertically.
3. Locate the two people asked about and read the relation from the tree.
4. Check: does the answer use only genders the question gave? If not, choose the gender-neutral option or "cannot be determined".

## Problem Patterns

### Pattern 1: Direct chain

**Example:** A is B's brother; B is C's mother → A is C's maternal uncle.

### Pattern 2: Pointing to a photo or person

**Example:** Pointing to a man, Neha, a woman, said, "His mother is the only daughter of my mother." Neha is a daughter of her mother, and her mother has only one daughter, so that daughter is Neha. Neha is the man's mother → the man is **Neha's son**. (If the speaker were male, the only daughter would be his sister, and the man would be his nephew — gender changes the answer.)

### Pattern 3: Coded relations

**Example:** P × Q: P is the brother of Q; P + Q: P is the mother of Q; P ÷ Q: P is the father of Q. Then A + B ÷ C × D means A is the mother of B, B is the father of C, C is the brother of D → A is the paternal **grandmother** of D.

### Pattern 4: Family puzzles

**Recognise it:** several people and several clues; questions about different pairs.

**Approach:** identify couples first (husband/wife clues), then attach children and grandparents.

## Common Mistakes

- Inferring gender from a name.
- Skipping a generation ("father's father" is grandfather, not father).
- Treating "only son" as "only child".
- Confusing paternal and maternal.
- Forgetting that "brother-in-law" has two meanings (spouse's brother or sister's husband).

## Placement Tips

- Draw even for "easy" questions — it takes seconds and prevents reversals ("A's uncle" vs "A is uncle").
- Read the question's direction carefully: "How is A related to B?" asks what A is **to** B.
- When options include both "brother" and "sister" and gender is not given, look for "cannot be determined" or a neutral option.

## Key Takeaways

- Draw a tree: generations vertical, spouses and siblings horizontal.
- Gender comes only from the words of the question.
- Replace long phrases from the inside out ("my grandfather's only son" → my father).
- Coded relations: translate symbols to words first.
