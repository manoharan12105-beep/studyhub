# Syllogisms — Practice

Every question uses the same five options. Accept the statements as true, however unusual.

### P1. Statements: All roses are flowers. All flowers are plants. Conclusions: I. All roses are plants. II. Some plants are roses.

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Pattern:** Chain of "All"

- A) Only I follows
- B) Only II follows
- C) Either I or II follows
- D) Neither I nor II follows
- E) Both I and II follow

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** E) Both I and II follow

**Explanation:** Roses ⊂ Flowers ⊂ Plants → I. Since roses exist and are plants, some plants are roses → II.

</details>

### P2. Statements: No pen is a pencil. All pencils are erasers. Conclusions: I. No pen is an eraser. II. Some erasers are not pens.

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Pattern:** Negative statements

- A) Only I follows
- B) Only II follows
- C) Either I or II follows
- D) Neither I nor II follows
- E) Both I and II follow

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) Only II follows

**Explanation:** The erasers that are pencils are not pens → II. Pens may still overlap erasers outside the pencil circle → I is not certain.

</details>

### P4. Statements: All doctors are graduates. Some graduates are engineers. Conclusions: I. Some doctors are engineers. II. No doctor is an engineer.

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Either–or

- A) Only I follows
- B) Only II follows
- C) Either I or II follows
- D) Neither I nor II follows
- E) Both I and II follow

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** C) Either I or II follows

**Explanation:** "All + Some" gives no definite doctor–engineer conclusion, so neither I nor II is certain. They form a complementary pair, so one must be true.

</details>

### P5. Statements: Some books are pens. No pen is a chair. Conclusions: I. Some books are not chairs. II. Some chairs are books.

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Negative statements

- B) Only II follows
- C) Either I or II follows
- D) Neither I nor II follows
- E) Both I and II follow

</details>

### P6. Statements: All apples are fruits. No fruit is a vegetable. Conclusions: I. No apple is a vegetable. II. Some vegetables being apples is a possibility.

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Possibility

- A) Only I follows
- B) Only II follows
- C) Either I or II follows
- D) Neither I nor II follows
- E) Both I and II follow

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** A) Only I follows

**Explanation:** Apples ⊂ Fruits, and fruits never overlap vegetables → I. That makes any apple–vegetable overlap impossible → II does not follow.

</details>

### P7. Statements: Some cats are dogs. Some dogs are rats. Conclusions: I. Some cats are rats. II. All rats being cats is a possibility.

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Possibility

- A) Only I follows
- B) Only II follows
- C) Either I or II follows
- D) Neither I nor II follows
- E) Both I and II follow

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) Only II follows

**Explanation:** "Some + Some" gives no definite cat–rat conclusion → I does not follow. A valid diagram can place all rats inside cats (with dogs overlapping both) → II follows.

</details>

### P8. Statements: All A are B. All C are B. Conclusions: I. Some A are C. II. No A is C.

### P9. Statements: No bird is a fish. Some fish are mammals. Conclusions: I. Some mammals are not birds. II. Some birds are mammals.

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Negative statements

- A) Only I follows
- B) Only II follows
- C) Either I or II follows
- D) Neither I nor II follows
- E) Both I and II follow

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** A) Only I follows

**Explanation:** The mammals that are fish cannot be birds → I. Birds may or may not overlap the other mammals → II is not certain. (I and II are not a complementary pair, so this is not an either–or case.)

</details>

### P10. Statements: All tables are chairs. All chairs are desks. Some desks are boxes. Conclusions: I. Some tables are desks. II. Some boxes are tables.

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Three statements

- A) Only I follows
- B) Only II follows
- C) Either I or II follows
- D) Neither I nor II follows
- E) Both I and II follow

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** A) Only I follows

**Explanation:** Tables ⊂ Chairs ⊂ Desks → all tables are desks → some tables are desks → I. The boxes overlap desks somewhere, not necessarily with tables → II is not certain.

</details>
