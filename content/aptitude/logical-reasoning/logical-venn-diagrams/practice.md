# Logical Venn Diagrams — Practice

### P1. Which diagram best represents *Dogs, Cats, Animals*?

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Pattern:** Choose the diagram

- A) Three separate circles
- B) Three nested circles
- C) Two separate circles inside a third
- D) Three overlapping circles

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** C) Two separate circles inside a third

**Explanation:** Dogs and cats share no members, and both are animals.

</details>

### P2. Which diagram best represents *Asia, India, Delhi*?

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Pattern:** Choose the diagram

- A) Three nested circles
- B) Two overlapping circles inside a third
- C) Three separate circles
- D) One circle inside another, third separate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** A) Three nested circles

**Explanation:** Delhi ⊂ India ⊂ Asia.

</details>

### P3. Which diagram best represents *Tables, Rivers, Cats*?

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Pattern:** Choose the diagram

- A) Three overlapping circles
- B) Three separate circles
- C) Two separate circles inside a third
- D) Three nested circles

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) Three separate circles

**Explanation:** No member belongs to two of these classes.

</details>

### P4. Which diagram best represents *Students, Players, Boys*?

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Choose the diagram

- A) Three nested circles
- B) Two separate circles inside a third
- C) Three mutually overlapping circles
- D) One circle inside another, third separate

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** C) Three mutually overlapping circles

**Explanation:** Some students are players, some players are boys, some boys are students, and some are all three — every pair overlaps, and none is contained in another.

</details>

### P5. Which diagram best represents *Cats, Animals, Tables*?

- C) Three nested circles
- D) Three separate circles

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** A) One circle inside another, third separate

**Explanation:** Cats ⊂ Animals; tables share nothing with either.

</details>

### P9. Of 60 people, 35 read newspaper A, 30 read newspaper B, and 8 read neither. How many read both?

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Formula questions

- A) 5
- B) 8
- C) 13
- D) 17

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** C) 13

**Explanation:** At least one = 60 − 8 = 52. Both = 35 + 30 − 52 = 13.

</details>

### P10. Of 120 people, 60 like tea, 50 like coffee and 40 like juice. 20 like tea and coffee, 15 coffee and juice, 10 tea and juice, and 5 like all three. How many like none of the three?

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Formula questions

- A) 5
- B) 10
- C) 15
- D) 20

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) 10

**Explanation:** At least one = 60 + 50 + 40 − 20 − 15 − 10 + 5 = 110 → none = 10. Option C forgets to add back the 5 who like all three.

</details>
