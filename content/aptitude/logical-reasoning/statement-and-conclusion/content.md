# Statement and Conclusion

## Concept

You are given a statement (a fact, a news item, a policy) and two conclusions. A conclusion **follows** if it can be logically derived from the statement — it must be true whenever the statement is true. You may use widely accepted common knowledge, but not personal opinion, guesses about the future, or facts not in the statement.

Think of it as: **"Given only this statement, am I forced to accept this conclusion?"** If the answer is "probably" or "maybe", the conclusion does not follow.

Answer options are standard:

| Option | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| A | Only conclusion I follows |
| B | Only conclusion II follows |
| C | Either I or II follows |
| D | Neither I nor II follows |
| E | Both I and II follow |

## Rules

### A conclusion follows when it

- restates the statement in other words, or is a direct consequence of it;
- follows from a stated rule applied to a stated case ("All employees get a bonus; Meena is an employee" → Meena gets a bonus);
- follows from a stated reason ("cancelled because of heavy rain" → it rained heavily).

### A conclusion does not follow when it

- **adds new information** the statement does not contain (reasons, motives, other facts);
- uses **extreme words** the statement does not support: *only, all, always, never, definitely, every*;
- **predicts the future** beyond what the statement says;
- **reverses a one-way rule** ("All toppers got scholarships; Ravi got a scholarship" does not mean Ravi was a topper);
- is a **value judgement** or opinion not implied by the statement.

### Useful logical forms

| Statement | Valid conclusion | Invalid conclusion |
|-----------|------------------|--------------------|
| All X are Y; A is X | A is Y | — |
| All X are Y; A is Y | — | A is X |
| All X are Y; A is not Y | A is not X | — |
| Only X can do Y; A did Y | A is X | All X will do Y |
| Most X are Y | Some X are Y | All X are Y |
| 60% of X are Y | 40% of X are not Y | anything about other groups |

### Either–or

"Either I or II" applies only when the two conclusions cover all possibilities and exactly one must be true — e.g. the statement says something will happen in one of two ways.

## Solving Approach

1. Read the statement and identify exactly what it asserts (facts, reasons, rules).
2. For each conclusion, ask: is it forced by the statement? Look for added facts, extreme words and future predictions.
3. Apply the logical forms above to rule-based statements.
4. Check for an either–or pair before choosing "neither".

## Problem Patterns

### Pattern 1: Rule applied to a case

**Example:** All employees of a firm get a year-end bonus; Meena is an employee → "Meena gets a year-end bonus" follows.

### Pattern 2: Reason given in the statement

**Example:** The train was cancelled because of heavy rain → "It rained heavily" follows; "Trains are always cancelled when it rains" does not (extreme word, generalisation).

### Pattern 3: "Only" statements

**Example:** Only those who pass the written test will be called for the interview → "No one who fails the written test will be called" follows; "Everyone who passes will be called" does not.

### Pattern 4: Percentages and "most"

**Example:** 60% of households own a two-wheeler → "40% do not" follows. "Most students passed" → "Some students passed" follows; "All passed" does not.

## Common Mistakes

- Accepting a conclusion because it is true in real life or sounds reasonable.
- Reversing one-way rules.
- Overlooking an extreme word in the conclusion.
- Treating possible causes or likely futures as conclusions.

## Placement Tips

- Underline extreme words in conclusions; they are usually the reason a conclusion fails.
- For long statements, focus on the key claim — most of the wording is context.
- Do not spend long on these; if a conclusion needs an extra assumption, it does not follow.

## Key Takeaways

- A conclusion must be forced by the statement alone.
- Reject added facts, extreme words, predictions and reversed rules.
- "Only X can do Y" and "All X are Y" are one-way; apply them in the stated direction only.
