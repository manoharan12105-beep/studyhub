# Calendars — Practice

### P1. How many odd days are there in a leap year?

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Pattern:** Odd days

- A) 0
- B) 1
- C) 2
- D) 3

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** C) 2

**Explanation:** 366 = 52 × 7 + 2.

</details>

### P2. Today is Wednesday. What day will it be after 100 days?

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Pattern:** Days after a given day

- A) Thursday
- B) Friday
- C) Saturday
- D) Sunday

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) Friday

**Explanation:** 100 mod 7 = 2 → Wednesday + 2 = Friday.

</details>

### P3. Which of these is a leap year?

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Pattern:** Leap years

- A) 1800
- B) 1900
- C) 2000
- D) 2100

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** C) 2000

**Explanation:** Century years must be divisible by 400; only 2000 is.

</details>

### P4. 5 March 2024 was a Tuesday. What day was 5 March 2025?

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Same date in another year

- A) Tuesday
- B) Wednesday
- C) Thursday
- D) Monday

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) Wednesday

**Explanation:** 29 February 2024 is before 5 March 2024, and February 2025 has 28 days → 365 days → +1. Option C wrongly adds a leap day.

</details>

### P5. How many odd days are there in 300 years?

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Odd days

- A) 0
- B) 1
- C) 3
- D) 5

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) 1

**Explanation:** 100 years → 5, so 300 years → 15 → 15 mod 7 = 1.

</details>

### P7. What day of the week was 1 January 2001?

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Day of a historical date

- A) Sunday
- B) Monday
- C) Tuesday
- D) Saturday

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) Monday

**Explanation:** 2000 complete years = 5 × 400 years → 0 odd days. 1 January adds 1 → Monday.

</details>

### P8. What day of the week was 2 October 1869?

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Day of a historical date

- A) Friday
- B) Saturday
- C) Sunday
- D) Thursday

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

1600 years → 0; 1601–1800 → 3; then count 1801–1868.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) Saturday

**Explanation:** 1601–1800 (200 years) → 3. 1801–1868: 68 years, 17 leap years → 51 + 34 = 85 → 1. 1869 up to 2 October (not leap): Jan 3, Feb 0, Mar 3, Apr 2, May 3, Jun 2, Jul 3, Aug 3, Sep 2, plus 2 → 23 → 2. Total = 0 + 3 + 1 + 2 = 6 → Saturday.

</details>

### P9. In which year will the calendar of 2023 next be repeated?

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Repeating calendar

- A) 2029
- B) 2030
- C) 2034
- D) 2051

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** C) 2034

**Explanation:** 2023 mod 4 = 3 → repeats after 11 years. Option A (6 years) applies only to years just after a leap year.

</details>

### P10. Which year has the same calendar as 2024?

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Repeating calendar

- A) 2030
- B) 2035
- C) 2048
- D) 2052

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** D) 2052

**Explanation:** A leap year's calendar repeats after 28 years (no century year in between).

</details>

### P11. A non-leap year begins on a Monday. How many Mondays does it have?

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Odd days

- A) 51
- B) 52
- C) 53
- D) 54

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** C) 53

**Explanation:** 365 = 52 weeks + 1 day. The extra day is the first day of the year, Monday, so Monday occurs 53 times.

</details>
