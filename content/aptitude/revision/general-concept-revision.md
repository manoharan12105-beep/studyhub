# Concept Revision

The core ideas behind every Aptitude topic — what to understand, not what to memorise. Formulas are in the Formula Sheet; speed techniques in Important Shortcuts.

## Quantitative Aptitude

### Number System

- Natural ⊂ Whole ⊂ Integers ⊂ Rational ⊂ Real. 1 is neither prime nor composite; 2 is the only even prime; there are 25 primes below 100.
- Divisibility rules work because powers of 10 leave fixed remainders (10 ≡ 1 mod 9, 10 ≡ −1 mod 11). Test composites with **co-prime** factors (12 = 3 × 4).
- Only perfect squares have an odd number of factors.
- Remainders can be added and multiplied piece by piece; negative remainders simplify work.
- Unit digits depend only on the last digits; power cycles have length 1, 2 or 4.

### Percentages, Profit and Loss

- Every percentage is "of" a base — the number after *of* or *than*.
- Percentage changes multiply; they never simply add.
- Profit and loss are measured on **cost price**; discount on **marked price**.

### Ratio, Averages, Ages, Mixtures

- A ratio a : b means quantities ax and bx — find x.
- Averages are easiest as totals: Sum = Average × Count.
- Age differences never change.
- Alligation is a weighted average solved backwards: the further an ingredient is from the mean, the less of it is needed.

### Interest

- Simple interest grows by the same amount each year (linear).
- Compound interest earns interest on interest — it is repeated percentage growth; first year SI = CI.

### Time and Work, Time-Speed-Distance

- Rates add, times do not. Use total work = LCM of times.
- Efficiency is inversely proportional to time.
- Pipes and leaks are positive and negative work.
- Relative speed: add for opposite directions, subtract for the same direction.
- A train crossing anything must cover its own length too.
- Downstream = boat + stream; upstream = boat − stream.

### Permutation, Combination and Probability

- Order matters → permutation; order doesn't → combination.
- AND → multiply; OR → add.
- Probability = favourable/total; "at least one" = 1 − P(none).
- Without replacement, later probabilities change.

### Algebra, Geometry, Mensuration

- Quadratics: the discriminant decides the nature of roots; the sum and product of roots come straight from coefficients.
- Multiplying an inequality by a negative number reverses it.
- Geometry questions chain two or three properties: angle sums, parallel lines, Pythagoras, circle angles.
- Melting and recasting conserves volume; scaling by k multiplies area by k² and volume by k³.

### Data Interpretation

- Read titles, units and axes before questions.
- DI is percentages, ratios and averages applied to data; watch the base year.
- Absolute change and percentage change can peak in different years.

## Logical Reasoning

### Series

- Number series: check differences → ratios → squares/cubes → mixed operations → alternate terms → special sequences.
- Alphabet series: convert letters to positions (EJOTY: E = 5, J = 10, O = 15, T = 20, Y = 25); opposite letters add to 27.
- Alphanumeric arrangements: number the positions first; "preceded by" = left neighbour.

### Coding-Decoding

- Most codes are shifts (constant, changing, alternating), reversals or opposite letters.
- In sentence coding, a word common to two sentences has the code common to those two sentences.

### Blood Relations and Direction Sense

- Draw a family tree: generations vertical, spouses and siblings horizontal. Gender comes **only** from the question's words, never from names.
- Directions: right = clockwise, left = anticlockwise, relative to the current facing. Morning shadows fall west; evening shadows east.

### Ranking, Analogy, Classification, Odd One Out

- A person counted from both ends is counted twice (−1).
- Analogies need a precise relationship with the same direction.
- Classification finds the most specific shared property; odd one out finds the item that breaks it.

### Syllogisms and Venn Diagrams

- A conclusion follows only if it is true in **every** valid diagram. "Some" means at least one, possibly all.
- All A are B converts to Some B are A; No A is B converts to No B is A; Some A are not B has no conversion.
- Either–or applies when neither of a complementary pair follows alone.
- Three classes: decide each pair (subset, separate, overlap), then choose the diagram.

### Statement-Based Reasoning

- **Conclusion:** must follow from the statement alone; reject extreme words, predictions, added facts.
- **Assumption:** what the statement takes for granted; use the negation test.
- **Cause and effect:** the cause comes first and explains the effect; simultaneous similar events suggest a common cause.

### Arrangements, Puzzles, Data Sufficiency

- Seating: facing the centre, right = anticlockwise; definite clues first, negative clues last.
- Puzzles: grid or table; branch into cases only when forced; verify all clues.
- Data sufficiency: test each statement alone before combining; sufficient = exactly one answer.

### Clocks and Calendars

- Minute hand 6°/min, hour hand 0.5°/min, gap changes 5.5°/min.
- Only odd days (days mod 7) matter. Century years are leap years only if divisible by 400.

## Verbal Ability

### Grammar

- **Parts of speech** are decided by the word's job in the sentence.
- **Subject–verb agreement:** ignore words between subject and verb; *each/every/either/neither/everyone* are singular; *or/nor* agree with the nearer subject; *a number of* is plural, *the number of* singular.
- **Tenses:** definite past time → simple past (never present perfect); *since* + point, *for* + period; stative verbs avoid continuous forms; future time clauses use the present.
- **Conditionals:** if + present → will; if + past → would; if + past perfect → would have + V3.
- **Articles:** a/an by sound (an hour, a university, an MBA); *the* for known, unique, superlative, rivers, instruments; no article for languages, meals, games, general plurals.
- **Prepositions:** at (time point), on (day), in (month/year); fixed pairs such as senior **to**, prefer **to**, different **from**; no preposition after *discuss, reach, enter*.
- **Pronouns:** object form after prepositions and *let*; *who* = subject, *whom* = object; singular collectives take *its*.
- **Conjunctions:** no sooner … than; hardly/scarcely … when; not only … but also; never although … but or unless … not.
- **Voice:** passive = object + be (same tense) + V3 + by + agent.
- **Reported speech:** past reporting verb → backshift; universal truths keep present; questions use statement order.

### Usage and Vocabulary

- Sentence correction and error spotting test a small set of errors: agreement, tense, pronouns, articles, prepositions, adjective/adverb, comparisons, parallelism, redundancy, modifiers.
- Fill in the blanks and sentence completion hinge on the clue word: contrast, cause, support or degree.
- Synonyms/antonyms: define first; eliminate traps (antonym among synonyms, synonym among antonyms).
- Roots (bene, mal, cred, spect, port, -cide, -phobia, omni-) unlock unfamiliar words.
- Idioms are not literal.

### Para Jumbles and Reading Comprehension

- Para jumbles: find the opening sentence and one mandatory pair (pronoun, connective, example, time word); eliminate options.
- RC: answer only from the passage; main idea = topic + author's point; inference = one small step; reject extreme and out-of-scope options.
