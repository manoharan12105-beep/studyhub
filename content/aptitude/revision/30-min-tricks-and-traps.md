# Tricks and Traps

The highest-value time-savers, the traps that catch most people, and how to use the last minutes before the test.

## Top tricks

| Trick | Use it for |
|-------|-----------|
| Take the base as 100 | Any percentage / profit question without actual numbers |
| Multiply factors (×1.2, ×0.8) | Successive changes, reverse percentages, markups |
| Fraction table (1/8 = 12.5%, 1/6 = 16.67%) | Fast percentages and DI |
| LCM as total work | Every time-and-work and pipe question |
| Alligation cross | Mixtures, two interest rates, two group averages |
| Unit digit / digit sum | Eliminating options before calculating |
| Glue / gap method | "Together" / "never together" arrangements |
| 1 − P(none) | "At least one" probability |
| Test the options | Ages, equations, ratio-change questions |
| Draw it | Directions, blood relations, seating, Venn diagrams |

> [!TIP]
> Before calculating anything, look at the options. Different unit digits, sizes or signs often settle the answer in seconds.

## Top traps

> [!WARNING]
> **Wrong base.** "A is 25% more than B" does not mean "B is 25% less than A" (it is 20%).

> [!WARNING]
> **Undoing a percentage.** After +20%, the original is X/1.2 — not X × 0.8.

> [!WARNING]
> **Same SP, equal % gain and loss** is always a net loss (x²/100 %).

> [!WARNING]
> **Average speed** over equal distances is 2xy/(x + y), not (x + y)/2.

> [!WARNING]
> **Unit mismatch.** Convert km/h to m/s (× 5/18) when lengths are in metres.

> [!WARNING]
> **Ranking −1.** Total = L + R − 1, and check the overlap case in "minimum" questions.

> [!WARNING]
> **Names are not genders.** In blood relations, use only the gender words in the question.

> [!WARNING]
> **Syllogism real-world bias.** Judge only from the statements, even if they are absurd.

> [!WARNING]
> **Extreme words** (*only, all, always, never*) usually make a conclusion, assumption or RC option wrong.

> [!WARNING]
> **Data sufficiency:** do not combine statements if one alone is enough; x² = 16 does not fix x.

> [!WARNING]
> **Grammar classics:** "each … have", "since five years", "I have seen him yesterday", "between you and I", "although … but", "more better".

## Exam-day strategy

- Attempt your strongest section first to build momentum.
- Give each question one honest attempt; if no approach appears within about 45 seconds, mark it and move on.
- In DI, seating and puzzle sets, judge the set before investing: a long setup only pays off if you will answer most of its questions.
- Keep 5 minutes at the end to return to marked questions.
- If there is negative marking, guess only when you can eliminate at least two options.
