# Profit, Loss and Discount — Solved Examples

### E1. Profit percentage

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Pattern:** Profit and loss percentage

**Problem:** A trader buys an item for ₹400 and sells it for ₹460. Find the profit percentage.

**Solution:**

1. Profit = 460 − 400 = ₹60.
2. Profit % = 60/400 × 100 = 15%.

**Answer:** **15%**

### E2. Cost price from selling price

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Pattern:** CP from SP

**Problem:** An item is sold for ₹640 at a loss of 20%. Find its cost price.

**Solution:**

1. A 20% loss means SP = 80% of CP.
2. CP = 640 × 100/80 = ₹800.

**Answer:** **₹800**

### E3. Markup followed by discount

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Markup and discount

**Problem:** A shopkeeper marks goods 25% above cost price and gives a discount of 12%. Find the profit percentage.

**Solution:**

1. Let CP = 100. MP = 125.
2. Discount = 12% of 125 = 15 → SP = 110.
3. Profit = 10 on CP 100 → 10%.

**Answer:** **10%**

> [!TIP]
> 1.25 × 0.88 = 1.10 in one step.

### E4. Successive discounts

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Successive discounts

**Problem:** Find the single discount equivalent to successive discounts of 20% and 10%.

**Solution:**

1. MP = 100. After 20%: 80.
2. After 10% of 80 (= 8): 72.
3. Total discount = 100 − 72 = 28%.

**Answer:** **28%**

### E5. Same selling price, profit on one and loss on the other

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Same SP, equal gain and loss

**Problem:** Two phones are sold for ₹9,900 each — one at a 10% profit and the other at a 10% loss. Find the overall profit or loss.

**Solution:**

1. Phone 1: CP = 9,900 × 100/110 = ₹9,000.
2. Phone 2: CP = 9,900 × 100/90 = ₹11,000.
3. Total CP = ₹20,000; total SP = ₹19,800.
4. Loss = ₹200 → 200/20,000 × 100 = 1%.

**Answer:** **Loss of ₹200 (1%)**

> [!TIP]
> x²/100 = 10²/100 = 1% loss, without computing the CPs.

### E6. CP of some articles equals SP of others

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** CP of x = SP of y

**Problem:** The cost price of 20 articles equals the selling price of 16 articles. Find the profit percentage.

**Solution:**

1. Let CP of one article = ₹1. CP of 20 = ₹20 = SP of 16.
2. SP of one = 20/16 = ₹1.25.
3. Profit per article = 0.25 on CP 1 → 25%.

**Answer:** **25% profit**

### E7. False weights

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Dishonest dealer

**Problem:** A shopkeeper claims to sell at cost price but uses a 900 g weight instead of 1 kg. Find his gain percentage.

**Solution:**

1. He charges the cost of 1000 g but gives only 900 g.
2. His cost is for 900 g; his extra receipt is the value of 100 g.
3. Gain % = 100/900 × 100 = 11.11%.

**Answer:** **11.11%** (exactly 11 1/9 %)

### E8. Required markup

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Markup and discount

**Problem:** A shopkeeper wants a 20% profit after giving a 10% discount. By what percentage above cost should he mark the goods?

**Solution:**

1. Let CP = 100. Required SP = 120.
2. SP is 90% of MP → MP = 120/0.9 = 133.33.
3. Markup = 33.33% above CP.

**Answer:** **33.33%** (33 1/3 %)
