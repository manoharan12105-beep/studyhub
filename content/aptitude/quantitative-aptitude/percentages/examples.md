# Percentages — Solved Examples

### E1. Percentage of a number

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Pattern:** Percentage of a number

**Problem:** Find 35% of 240.

**Solution:**

1. 10% of 240 = 24, so 30% = 72.
2. 5% = half of 10% = 12.
3. 35% = 72 + 12 = 84.

**Answer:** **84**

### E2. Percentage change

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Pattern:** Percentage change

**Problem:** The price of a book rises from ₹80 to ₹92. Find the percentage increase.

**Solution:**

1. Change = 92 − 80 = 12.
2. Base = old price = 80.
3. % increase = 12/80 × 100 = 15%.

**Answer:** **15%**

### E3. More than vs less than

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Comparing two quantities

**Problem:** A's salary is 25% more than B's. By what percent is B's salary less than A's?

**Solution:**

1. Let B = 100. Then A = 125.
2. Difference = 25.
3. "Less than A" uses A as the base: 25/125 × 100 = 20%.

**Answer:** **20%**

> [!TIP]
> Formula: r/(100 + r) × 100 = 25/125 × 100 = 20%.

### E4. Successive changes

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Successive changes

**Problem:** A price is first increased by 20% and then decreased by 20%. What is the net change?

**Solution:**

1. Let the price be 100.
2. After +20%: 120.
3. After −20% of 120 (= 24): 96.
4. Net change = 96 − 100 = −4 → 4% decrease.

**Answer:** **4% decrease**

> [!TIP]
> a + b + ab/100 = 20 − 20 − 400/100 = −4%.

### E5. Keeping expenditure constant

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Price and consumption

**Problem:** The price of sugar rises by 25%. By what percent must a family reduce consumption so that expenditure stays the same?

**Solution:**

1. Let price = 100 and consumption = 100 units → expenditure = 10,000.
2. New price = 125. For expenditure 10,000, consumption = 10,000 / 125 = 80 units.
3. Reduction = 20 units out of 100 = 20%.

**Answer:** **20%**

### E6. Election with invalid votes

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Elections

**Problem:** In an election between two candidates, 10% of the votes cast were invalid. The winner got 58% of the valid votes and won by 1,440 votes. How many votes were cast?

**Solution:**

1. Loser's share of valid votes = 100 − 58 = 42%.
2. Margin = 58% − 42% = 16% of valid votes = 1,440 → valid votes = 1,440 / 0.16 = 9,000.
3. Valid votes are 90% of votes cast → votes cast = 9,000 / 0.9 = 10,000.

**Answer:** **10,000**

### E7. Pass marks

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Pass marks

**Problem:** A student scoring 30% fails by 15 marks. Another student scoring 40% gets 35 marks more than the pass mark. Find the maximum marks and the pass mark.

**Solution:**

1. Let maximum marks = M. Pass mark = 0.30M + 15 = 0.40M − 35.
2. 0.10M = 50 → M = 500.
3. Pass mark = 0.30 × 500 + 15 = 165.
4. Check with the second student: 0.40 × 500 = 200 = 165 + 35 ✓.

**Answer:** Maximum marks **500**, pass mark **165**.

### E8. Population with different yearly rates

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Growth and depreciation

**Problem:** A town's population is 50,000. It increases by 10% in the first year, decreases by 10% in the second, and increases by 20% in the third. Find the population after 3 years.

**Solution:**

1. Multiply by each year's factor: 50,000 × 1.1 × 0.9 × 1.2.
2. 50,000 × 1.1 = 55,000.
3. 55,000 × 0.9 = 49,500.
4. 49,500 × 1.2 = 59,400.

**Answer:** **59,400**

> [!WARNING]
> Adding the rates (10 − 10 + 20 = 20%) would give 60,000 — a typical wrong option.
