# Probability — Practice

### P1. A die is rolled. What is the probability of getting a number greater than 4?

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Pattern:** Dice

- A) 1/6
- B) 1/3
- C) 1/2
- D) 2/3

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) 1/3

**Explanation:** Favourable: 5, 6 → 2/6 = 1/3.

</details>

### P2. A card is drawn from a deck of 52. What is the probability that it is a face card (J, Q or K)?

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Pattern:** Cards

- A) 1/13
- B) 3/13
- C) 4/13
- D) 3/52

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) 3/13

**Explanation:** 3 face cards × 4 suits = 12 → 12/52 = 3/13.

</details>

### P6. A bag contains 4 white and 6 black balls. Two balls are drawn at random. What is the probability that one is white and one is black?

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Balls from a bag

- A) 4/15
- B) 2/5
- C) 8/15
- D) 12/25

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** C) 8/15

**Explanation:** Favourable: 4 × 6 = 24; total ¹⁰C₂ = 45 → 24/45 = 8/15. Option D assumes replacement (2 × 0.4 × 0.6).

</details>

### P7. A speaks the truth in 3 out of 4 cases and B in 4 out of 5 cases. What is the probability that they contradict each other when describing the same event?

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Truth and contradiction

- A) 3/20
- B) 7/20
- C) 3/5
- D) 13/20

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) 7/20

**Explanation:** Exactly one tells the truth: (3/4)(1/5) + (1/4)(4/5) = 3/20 + 4/20 = 7/20. Option D is the probability that they agree.

</details>

### P8. A letter is chosen at random from the word EXAMINATION. What is the probability that it is a vowel?

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Counting outcomes

- A) 4/11
- B) 5/11
- C) 6/11
- D) 1/2

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** C) 6/11

**Explanation:** 11 letters: E, X, A, M, I, N, A, T, I, O, N. Vowels: E, A, I, A, I, O → 6. Each letter position counts, so repeated vowels count twice. Option A counts only distinct vowels (E, A, I, O).

</details>

### P10. What is the probability that a leap year, chosen at random, has 53 Sundays?

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Calendar probability

- A) 1/7
- B) 2/7
- C) 3/7
- D) 1/53

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) 2/7

**Explanation:** 366 days = 52 weeks + 2 days. The two extra consecutive days are one of 7 equally likely pairs; Sat–Sun and Sun–Mon contain Sunday → 2/7. Option A is for a non-leap year.

</details>

### P11. A coin is tossed 4 times. What is the probability of getting at least one head?

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Complement

- A) 1/2
- B) 3/4
- C) 7/8
- D) 15/16

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** D) 15/16

**Explanation:** P(no heads) = (1/2)⁴ = 1/16 → 1 − 1/16 = 15/16.

</details>

**Explanation:** Favourable: ⁴C₂ × ⁵C₁ = 6 × 5 = 30. Total: ⁹C₃ = 84. P = 30/84 = 5/14.

</details>

### P13. Three dice are rolled. What is the probability that all three show the same number?

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Dice

- A) 1/216
- B) 1/72
- C) 1/36
- D) 1/6

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** C) 1/36

**Explanation:** Total 6³ = 216. Favourable: (1,1,1) … (6,6,6) → 6. 6/216 = 1/36. Option A counts only one specific number (e.g. three sixes).

</details>
