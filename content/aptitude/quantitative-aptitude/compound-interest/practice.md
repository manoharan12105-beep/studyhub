# Compound Interest — Practice

### P1. Find the compound interest on ₹5,000 at 10% per annum for 2 years, compounded annually.

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Pattern:** Find CI

- A) ₹1,000
- B) ₹1,050
- C) ₹1,100
- D) ₹6,050

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) ₹1,050

**Explanation:** 2 years at 10% = 21% → 21% of 5,000 = 1,050. Option A is SI; option D is the amount.

</details>

### P2. What will ₹8,000 amount to in 2 years at 5% per annum compound interest?

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Pattern:** Find amount

- A) ₹8,800
- B) ₹8,810
- C) ₹8,820
- D) ₹8,840

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** C) ₹8,820

**Explanation:** 8,000 × 1.05² = 8,000 × 1.1025 = 8,820.

</details>

### P3. A sum is invested at 20% per annum compound interest for 2 years. By what percentage does it grow in total?

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Pattern:** Effective rate

- A) 40%
- B) 42%
- C) 44%
- D) 48%

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** C) 44%

**Explanation:** 20 + 20 + (20 × 20)/100 = 44%, or 1.2² = 1.44.

</details>

### P5. What is the difference between compound and simple interest on ₹12,000 for 2 years at 5% per annum?

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** CI − SI

- A) ₹25
- B) ₹30
- C) ₹60
- D) ₹120

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) ₹30

**Explanation:** P × (R/100)² = 12,000 × 0.0025 = 30.

</details>

### P6. A sum becomes three times itself in 4 years at compound interest. In how many years will it become nine times itself?

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Growth multiples

- A) 8
- B) 12
- C) 16
- D) 36

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** A) 8

**Explanation:** 9 = 3², so it takes two 4-year periods = 8 years. Option B is the linear (SI-style) answer.

</details>

### P7. A sum amounts to ₹2,420 in 2 years and to ₹2,662 in 3 years at compound interest. Find the sum.

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Consecutive amounts

- A) ₹1,800
- B) ₹2,000
- C) ₹2,100
- D) ₹2,200

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) ₹2,000

**Explanation:** R = (2,662 − 2,420)/2,420 × 100 = 10%. P = 2,420/1.21 = 2,000.

</details>

### P9. The difference between CI and SI on a sum for 3 years at 10% per annum is ₹93. Find the sum.

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** CI − SI

- A) ₹3,000
- B) ₹3,100
- C) ₹9,300
- D) ₹31,000

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** A) ₹3,000

**Explanation:** P × 0.01 × (3 + 0.1) = 0.031P = 93 → P = 3,000. Option C uses the 2-year formula.

</details>

### P10. A loan of ₹8,200 at 5% per annum compound interest is repaid in two equal annual instalments. Find each instalment.

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Instalments

- A) ₹4,100
- B) ₹4,305
- C) ₹4,410
- D) ₹4,520.25

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

x/1.05 + x/1.05² = 8,200.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** C) ₹4,410

**Explanation:** x/1.05 + x/1.1025 = 8,200. With x = 4,410: 4,200 + 4,000 = 8,200 ✓. Option D divides the 2-year amount (8,200 × 1.1025) equally, which overcharges.

</details>

### P11. Find the compound interest on ₹16,000 for 9 months at 20% per annum, compounded quarterly.

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Quarterly compounding

- A) ₹2,400
- B) ₹2,522
- C) ₹2,600
- D) ₹2,648

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) ₹2,522

**Explanation:** 5% per quarter for 3 quarters: 16,000 × 1.05³ = 16,000 × 1.157625 = 18,522 → CI = 2,522. Option A is simple interest.

</details>

### P12. The compound interest on a sum for 2 years is ₹832 and the simple interest for the same period at the same rate is ₹800. Find the sum.

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** CI and SI both given

- A) ₹4,000
- B) ₹5,000
- C) ₹6,400
- D) ₹8,000

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) ₹5,000

**Explanation:** One year's SI = 400. The extra ₹32 is interest on 400 for one year → R = 8%. P = 400/0.08 = 5,000. Check: 5,000 × 1.08² − 5,000 = 832 ✓.

</details>
