# Data Interpretation — Practice

DI questions come in sets that share one data source, so this page is grouped by set. Within each set, questions go from easier to harder.

## Set 1: Table

Books sold by a store (in hundreds):

| Month | Fiction | Science | History |
|-------|---------|---------|---------|
| Jan | 25 | 15 | 10 |
| Feb | 30 | 18 | 12 |
| Mar | 20 | 22 | 8 |
| Apr | 35 | 25 | 16 |

### P1. How many books were sold in February?

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Pattern:** Tables

- A) 600
- B) 6,000
- C) 5,000
- D) 60

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) 6,000

**Explanation:** 30 + 18 + 12 = 60 hundred = 6,000. Option D ignores the unit "in hundreds".

</details>

### P2. What is the average number of Fiction books sold per month?

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Pattern:** Tables

- A) 2,500
- B) 2,750
- C) 2,800
- D) 3,000

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) 2,750

**Explanation:** (25 + 30 + 20 + 35)/4 = 110/4 = 27.5 hundred = 2,750.

</details>

### P3. By what percentage did Science sales increase from January to April?

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Tables

- A) 40%
- B) 60%
- C) 66.67%
- D) 10%

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** C) 66.67%

**Explanation:** (25 − 15)/15 × 100 = 66.67%. Option A divides by the April value.

</details>

### P4. In which month was History's share of total sales the highest?

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Tables

- A) Jan
- B) Feb
- C) Mar
- D) Apr

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** D) Apr

**Explanation:** Totals: Jan 50, Feb 60, Mar 50, Apr 76. Shares: 20%, 20%, 16%, 16/76 ≈ 21.05% → April.

</details>

## Set 2: Bar Graph

### P5. Sales in 2020 were approximately what percentage of sales in 2022?

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Bar Graphs

- A) 55%
- B) 64%
- C) 69%
- D) 156%

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) 64%

**Explanation:** 135/210 × 100 ≈ 64.3%. Option D is 210/135.

</details>

### P6. By what percentage did sales fall from 2019 to 2020?

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Bar Graphs

- A) 10%
- B) 11.11%
- C) 15%
- D) 12.5%

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** A) 10%

**Explanation:** (150 − 135)/150 × 100 = 10%. Option B divides by the 2020 value.

</details>

### P7. If sales in 2023 grow at the same percentage rate as from 2021 to 2022, what will they be?

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Bar Graphs

- A) ₹235 crore
- B) ₹240 crore
- C) ₹245 crore
- D) ₹250 crore

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** C) ₹245 crore

**Explanation:** Growth 2021 → 2022 = 30/180 = 1/6. 210 × 7/6 = 245. Option B adds the same absolute increase (30).

</details>

## Set 3: Line Graph

![Line graph of Company Y income and expenditure in rupees lakh from 2018 to 2022. Income: 50, 60, 55, 70, 80. Expenditure: 40, 45, 50, 52, 60](images/line-graph-income-expenditure.svg)

### P8. What is the total profit (income − expenditure) over the five years?

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Line Graphs

- A) ₹58 lakh
- B) ₹63 lakh
- C) ₹68 lakh
- D) ₹72 lakh

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** C) ₹68 lakh

**Explanation:** 10 + 15 + 5 + 18 + 20 = 68. (Or 315 − 247 = 68.)

</details>

### P9. In how many years was expenditure **more than** 80% of income?

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Line Graphs

- A) 0
- B) 1
- C) 2
- D) 3

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) 1

**Explanation:** Expenditure/income: 2018 40/50 = 80% (not more), 2019 75%, 2020 50/55 ≈ 90.9%, 2021 ≈ 74.3%, 2022 75%. Only 2020.

</details>

## Set 4: Pie Chart

![Pie chart of a family monthly budget of 50,000 rupees: Food 30%, Rent 25%, Education 15%, Transport 10%, Savings 12%, Others 8%](images/pie-chart-budget.svg)

### P10. How much does the family spend on rent and transport together?

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Pie Charts

- A) ₹15,000
- B) ₹17,500
- C) ₹18,000
- D) ₹20,000

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) ₹17,500

**Explanation:** 25% + 10% = 35% of 50,000 = 17,500.

</details>

### P11. The family's income rises by 20%, and the savings sector keeps the same central angle. By how much do monthly savings increase?

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Pie Charts

- A) ₹1,000
- B) ₹1,200
- C) ₹1,440
- D) ₹6,000

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) ₹1,200

**Explanation:** Same angle → same 12% share. Old savings = 12% of 50,000 = 6,000; new = 12% of 60,000 = 7,200 → increase 1,200.

</details>

## Set 5: Caselet

A survey of 600 people found that 55% drink tea, 45% drink coffee, and 15% drink both.

### P12. How many people drink neither tea nor coffee?

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Caselet DI

- A) 0
- B) 60
- C) 90
- D) 150

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Tea or coffee = tea + coffee − both.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** C) 90

**Explanation:** Tea or coffee = 55 + 45 − 15 = 85% → neither = 15% of 600 = 90. Option A assumes 55% + 45% = 100% covers everyone, ignoring the overlap.

</details>

### P13. How many people drink exactly one of the two beverages?

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Caselet DI

- A) 330
- B) 420
- C) 510
- D) 600

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) 420

**Explanation:** Tea only = 55 − 15 = 40%; coffee only = 45 − 15 = 30% → 70% of 600 = 420.

</details>
