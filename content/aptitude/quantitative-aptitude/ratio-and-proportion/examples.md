# Ratio and Proportion — Solved Examples

### E1. Dividing an amount

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Pattern:** Dividing in a ratio

**Problem:** Divide ₹1,200 between A and B in the ratio 3 : 5.

**Solution:**

1. Total parts = 3 + 5 = 8.
2. One part = 1,200 / 8 = 150.
3. A = 3 × 150 = 450; B = 5 × 150 = 750.

**Answer:** A = **₹450**, B = **₹750**

### E2. Combining two ratios

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Pattern:** Combining ratios

**Problem:** A : B = 2 : 3 and B : C = 4 : 5. Find A : B : C.

**Solution:**

1. B is 3 in the first ratio and 4 in the second. LCM(3, 4) = 12.
2. First ratio × 4 → A : B = 8 : 12.
3. Second ratio × 3 → B : C = 12 : 15.
4. A : B : C = 8 : 12 : 15.

**Answer:** **8 : 12 : 15**

### E3. Proportionals

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Mean, third and fourth proportional

**Problem:** Find (a) the mean proportional of 9 and 16, (b) the third proportional to 4 and 6, (c) the fourth proportional to 4, 6 and 10.

**Solution:**

1. (a) √(9 × 16) = √144 = 12. Check: 9 : 12 = 12 : 16 (both 3 : 4).
2. (b) 6² / 4 = 36/4 = 9. Check: 4 : 6 = 6 : 9 (both 2 : 3).
3. (c) (6 × 10)/4 = 15. Check: 4 : 6 = 10 : 15 (both 2 : 3).

**Answer:** (a) **12** (b) **9** (c) **15**

### E4. Adding to change a ratio

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Change in a ratio

**Problem:** What number must be added to both 7 and 11 so that the ratio becomes 3 : 4?

**Solution:**

1. (7 + x)/(11 + x) = 3/4.
2. 4(7 + x) = 3(11 + x) → 28 + 4x = 33 + 3x → x = 5.
3. Check: 12 : 16 = 3 : 4 ✓.

**Answer:** **5**

### E5. Coins of different values

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Count ratio vs value ratio

**Problem:** A bag has ₹1, 50 paise and 25 paise coins in the ratio 5 : 6 : 8 by number. The total value is ₹210. How many coins of each kind are there?

**Solution:**

1. Value ratio = 5 × 1 : 6 × 0.50 : 8 × 0.25 = 5 : 3 : 2.
2. Total value parts = 10 → one part = 210/10 = ₹21.
3. Values: ₹105, ₹63, ₹42.
4. Counts: 105/1 = 105; 63/0.5 = 126; 42/0.25 = 168.
5. Check: 105 : 126 : 168 = 5 : 6 : 8 (each ÷ 21) ✓.

**Answer:** **105**, **126** and **168** coins.

### E6. Joining changes the ratio

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Change in a ratio

**Problem:** The ratio of boys to girls in a class is 7 : 5. When 8 more girls join, the ratio becomes 7 : 6. How many boys are there?

**Solution:**

1. Boys = 7x, girls = 5x.
2. 7x / (5x + 8) = 7/6 → 42x = 35x + 56 → x = 8.
3. Boys = 7 × 8 = 56 (the number of boys did not change).

**Answer:** **56**

### E7. Incomes, expenditures and savings

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Income, expenditure and savings

**Problem:** The incomes of A and B are in the ratio 4 : 3 and their expenditures in the ratio 3 : 2. If each saves ₹6,000, find their incomes.

**Solution:**

1. Incomes 4x, 3x; expenditures 3y, 2y.
2. 4x − 3y = 6000 … (1) and 3x − 2y = 6000 … (2).
3. (1) × 2: 8x − 6y = 12000. (2) × 3: 9x − 6y = 18000.
4. Subtract: x = 6000. From (2): 18000 − 2y = 6000 → y = 6000.
5. Incomes = 4 × 6000 = ₹24,000 and 3 × 6000 = ₹18,000. Expenditures ₹18,000 and ₹12,000 — both save ₹6,000 ✓.

**Answer:** A = **₹24,000**, B = **₹18,000**
