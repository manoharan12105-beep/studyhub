# Active and Passive Voice

## Concept

In the **active voice**, the subject does the action: "The cat **chased** the mouse." In the **passive voice**, the subject receives the action: "The mouse **was chased** by the cat."

The passive is useful when the doer is unknown, unimportant or obvious ("My bag **has been stolen**"), or when the receiver is the focus. Only verbs with an **object** (transitive verbs) can be made passive — "She sleeps" has no passive.

The conversion has a fixed recipe; tests check that you keep the **tense** the same and change pronouns correctly.

## Rules

### The recipe

```text
Active:   Subject + verb + Object
Passive:  Object  + (form of "be" in the same tense) + past participle + by + Subject
```

1. The object becomes the new subject.
2. Use the form of **be** that matches the original tense.
3. Add the **past participle** (V3) of the main verb.
4. Move the original subject into a "by" phrase (or drop it if it is vague: *someone, people, they*).
5. Change pronoun case: *I → me, he → him, they → them*.

### Tense table

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|-------|--------|---------|
| Simple present | She writes a letter. | A letter **is written** by her. |
| Present continuous | She is writing a letter. | A letter **is being written** by her. |
| Present perfect | She has written a letter. | A letter **has been written** by her. |
| Simple past | She wrote a letter. | A letter **was written** by her. |
| Past continuous | She was writing a letter. | A letter **was being written** by her. |
| Past perfect | She had written a letter. | A letter **had been written** by her. |
| Simple future | She will write a letter. | A letter **will be written** by her. |
| Future perfect | She will have written a letter. | A letter **will have been written** by her. |
| Modals | She can/must/should write a letter. | A letter **can/must/should be written** by her. |

Perfect continuous tenses and the future continuous are rarely put in the passive.

### Special cases

- **Questions:** "Who wrote this poem?" → "**By whom was** this poem written?" "Did she write it?" → "**Was it written** by her?"
- **Commands:** "Close the door." → "**Let** the door **be closed**." Polite requests: "Please help me." → "You **are requested to** help me."
- **Two objects:** "The teacher gave the students a test." → "The students **were given** a test by the teacher." or "A test **was given** to the students by the teacher."
- **Vague subjects:** "Someone has stolen my bag." → "My bag **has been stolen**." (drop "by someone").
- **"People say that…":** → "It **is said** that he is honest." or "He **is said to be** honest."

## Solving Approach

1. Identify the tense of the active verb.
2. Make the object the subject and pick the matching form of *be*.
3. Add V3, then "by" + object form of the doer.
4. Check that the tense in the answer is the same as in the question.

## Common Mistakes

- Changing the tense ("She wrote" → "was being written").
- Using the subject pronoun after "by" ("by she" ✗ → "by her").
- Forgetting "being" in continuous tenses or "been" in perfect tenses.
- Trying to make intransitive verbs passive.

## Placement Tips

- Find the tense first; then the options with the wrong form of *be* can be eliminated at once.
- For questions and commands, memorise the "By whom…" and "Let … be …" patterns.

## Key Takeaways

- Passive = object + be (same tense) + V3 + by + agent.
- Continuous → being; perfect → been; modal → modal + be.
- Questions: By whom …? Commands: Let … be + V3.
- Drop vague agents (someone, people).
