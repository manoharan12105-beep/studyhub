# Para Jumbles

## Concept

A **para jumble** gives the sentences of a short paragraph in a scrambled order (usually labelled P, Q, R, S). You must find the order that makes a coherent paragraph.

A well-written paragraph is held together by **links** between sentences: a pronoun pointing back to a noun, a connective (*however, therefore, then*), a time sequence, or a general statement followed by an example. Solving a jumble means **finding these links**, not reading every possible order.

## Rules

### Finding the opening sentence

The first sentence usually:

- introduces the topic or names the main subject in full (*The United Nations (UN) is…*);
- does **not** begin with a pronoun that refers back (*It, This, They, Such*);
- does **not** begin with a connective that needs a previous idea (*However, Therefore, Also, Then, For example*).

### Mandatory pairs (links)

| Link type | Signal | Rule |
|-----------|--------|------|
| Pronoun / reference | it, this, they, such, these, the method | Comes **after** the sentence that names the noun |
| Contrast | however, but, yet, on the other hand | Comes after the idea it contrasts |
| Result | therefore, as a result, so, thus | Comes after the cause |
| Example | for example, for instance, one such | Comes after the general statement |
| Time / sequence | first, then, next, later, finally, today | Follows chronological order |
| Full form → short form | United Nations (UN) … the UN | Full form first |

### Finding the closing sentence

The last sentence often concludes or looks ahead: *therefore, thus, in short, today, finally*.

## Solving Approach

1. Find the opening sentence.
2. Find one strong mandatory pair (pronoun or connective).
3. **Use the options:** eliminate every option that breaks your opening or your pair — usually only one or two remain.
4. Check the remaining order by reading it through once.

**Example:**

- P. However, it took centuries for the idea to become popular.
- Q. The idea of a printed book first appeared in China.
- R. Today, printed books are found in every corner of the world.
- S. Later, Gutenberg's press made printing much faster in Europe.

Q introduces "the idea"; P ("However, … the idea") must follow Q; S ("Later") continues the history; R ("Today") ends → **QPSR**.

## Common Mistakes

- Starting with a sentence that begins with a pronoun or connective.
- Ignoring the options — testing all 24 orders of four sentences is too slow.
- Choosing an order that reads smoothly in parts but breaks a pronoun link elsewhere.

## Placement Tips

- One opening sentence plus one mandatory pair usually leaves a single option.
- Time words (*first, then, later, today*) give the whole order quickly in narrative jumbles.

## Key Takeaways

- Opening: introduces the subject; no back-referring pronoun or connective.
- Mandatory pairs: pronouns, connectives, examples, sequence words.
- Eliminate options early; confirm by reading the chosen order.
