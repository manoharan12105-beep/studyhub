# Idioms and Phrases

## Concept

An **idiom** is a group of words whose meaning is different from the meanings of the individual words: "It's **a piece of cake**" means "it's very easy", not anything about cake. Idiom questions give the expression (often inside a sentence) and ask for its meaning.

Because idioms are not literal, reasoning from the words alone often fails. The reliable approach is **knowing the common idioms** and using the sentence context when you don't.

## Rules

### Common idioms

| Idiom | Meaning | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| A piece of cake | Very easy | The test was a piece of cake. |
| Break the ice | Start a conversation in a tense or new situation | He told a joke to break the ice. |
| Hit the nail on the head | Describe exactly what is right | You hit the nail on the head with that analysis. |
| Once in a blue moon | Very rarely | He visits us once in a blue moon. |

| Cost an arm and a leg | Be very expensive | That phone cost an arm and a leg. |

| A blessing in disguise | Something that seems bad but turns out good | Missing the bus was a blessing in disguise. |
| Beat around the bush | Avoid the main point | Stop beating around the bush and tell me. |

| See eye to eye | Agree | They rarely see eye to eye. |

## Solving Approach

1. If you know the idiom, choose its standard meaning.
2. If not, read the sentence: is the idea positive or negative? About speed, secrecy, trouble, agreement?
3. Eliminate literal interpretations — the answer is almost never the literal meaning.

## Common Mistakes

- Choosing the literal meaning.
- Confusing similar idioms (*spill the beans* vs *let the cat out of the bag* both mean revealing a secret; *bury the hatchet* means making peace, not hiding something).

## Placement Tips

- Learn idioms in small batches with an example sentence each; the sentence is what you remember.
- Group idioms by theme (secrets, trouble, ease, timing) to recall them faster.

## Key Takeaways

- Idioms are not literal; know the common ones.
- Use sentence tone and context when unsure.
- Reject literal options.
