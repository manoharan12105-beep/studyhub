# Vocabulary Building

## Concept

A strong vocabulary is the base for [Synonyms](../synonyms/content.md), [Antonyms](../antonyms/content.md), [One Word Substitution](../one-word-substitution/content.md), [Idioms](../idioms-and-phrases/content.md), [Fill in the Blanks](../fill-in-the-blanks/content.md) and [Reading Comprehension](../reading-comprehension/content.md). Vocabulary cannot be crammed in a week, but it can be built efficiently with three tools:

1. **Roots, prefixes and suffixes** — one root explains many words.
2. **Context** — learning words inside sentences, not as isolated definitions.
3. **Spaced review** — revisiting words at increasing intervals until they stick.

This topic also covers **commonly confused words**, which appear often in error-spotting and fill-in-the-blank questions.

### Commonly confused words

| Pair | Difference |
|------|------------|
| affect / effect | *affect* (verb) = to influence; *effect* (noun) = a result |
| accept / except | *accept* = to receive; *except* = excluding |
| advice / advise | *advice* (noun); *advise* (verb) |
| principal / principle | *principal* = head of a school, or main; *principle* = a rule or belief |
| stationary / stationery | *stationary* = not moving; *stationery* = writing materials |
| complement / compliment | *complement* = to complete; *compliment* = praise |
| lose / loose | *lose* = to fail to keep; *loose* = not tight |
| emigrate / immigrate | *emigrate* = leave one's country; *immigrate* = come into a new country |
| eminent / imminent | *eminent* = distinguished; *imminent* = about to happen |
| council / counsel | *council* = an advisory or governing body; *counsel* = advice, or to advise |
| economic / economical | *economic* = relating to the economy; *economical* = thrifty, not wasteful |
| historic / historical | *historic* = important in history; *historical* = relating to history |
| continual / continuous | *continual* = repeated with breaks; *continuous* = without a break |
| imply / infer | *imply* = suggest indirectly (speaker); *infer* = conclude (listener) |
| ensure / insure | *ensure* = make certain; *insure* = protect with insurance |
| farther / further | *farther* = physical distance; *further* = additional, or abstract extent |
| lie / lay | *lie* (lie–lay–lain) = to recline, no object; *lay* (lay–laid–laid) = to put something down |

### A study method that works

1. **Read daily** — editorials, articles, good non-fiction. Note unfamiliar words with the sentence they appeared in.
2. **Record** each word with its meaning, one synonym, one antonym, and your own example sentence.
3. **Review** after 1 day, 3 days, 1 week and 1 month (spaced repetition).
4. **Use** new words in your own writing or speech within a week.
5. **Group** words by root or theme so they reinforce each other.

## Problem Patterns

### Pattern 1: Meaning from a root

**Example:** "incredible" → *in-* (not) + *cred* (believe) + *-ible* (can be) → cannot be believed.

### Pattern 2: Choosing between confused words

**Example:** "The new policy will ___ thousands of workers." → a verb meaning "influence" → **affect**.

## Common Mistakes

- Memorising lists without example sentences — the words fade quickly.
- Mixing up near-identical spellings (stationary/stationery, principal/principle).
- Over-relying on roots: some words have drifted from their roots' meanings; confirm with context.

## Placement Tips

- Ten new words a day with spaced review builds a strong vocabulary in a few months.
- Confused-word pairs are quick marks in error spotting — learn the table.

## Key Takeaways

- Roots and affixes unlock families of words.
- Learn words in context and review them on a spaced schedule.
- Know the confused pairs: affect/effect, principal/principle, stationary/stationery, imply/infer.
