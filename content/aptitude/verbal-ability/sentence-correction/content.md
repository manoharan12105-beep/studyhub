# Sentence Correction

## Concept

A sentence is given with one part highlighted; you choose the replacement that makes the sentence correct — or "No improvement" if it is already correct. Unlike [Error Spotting](../error-spotting/content.md), you must also judge **which fix is best**, so a good answer is grammatical, keeps the meaning, and is clear and concise.

Almost every sentence-correction question tests one of about ten error types. Recognising the type is most of the work.

## Rules

### The common error types

| Error type | Wrong | Right |
|------------|-------|-------|
| Subject–verb agreement | She **don't** like coffee. | She **doesn't** like coffee. |
| Tense | I **have seen** him yesterday. | I **saw** him yesterday. |
| Since / for | working here **since five years** | working here **for five years** |
| Pronoun case | between you and **I** | between you and **me** |
| Redundancy | He **returned back** home. | He **returned** home. |
| Fewer / less | **less** students | **fewer** students (countable) |
| Double comparative/superlative | **most cleverest** | **cleverest** |
| Illogical comparison | greater than **Mumbai** | greater than **that of Mumbai** |
| Parallelism | reading, writing and **to paint** | reading, writing and **painting** |
| Dangling modifier | **Walking down the street, the trees** looked beautiful. | **Walking down the street, I** found the trees beautiful. |
| Fixed pairs | **Hardly** had he left **than** | **Hardly** had he left **when** |

### Redundant expressions to watch

*return back, repeat again, revert back, combine together, each and every, free gift, past history, future plans, final outcome, true facts, cousin brother/sister* — the extra word adds nothing.

### Comparisons

- Compare like with like: "The climate of Delhi is hotter than **that of** Shimla."
- Two items → comparative (*better, taller*); three or more → superlative (*best, tallest*).
- Never combine *more/most* with *-er/-est*.

### Modifiers

A modifying phrase at the start of a sentence must describe the subject that follows it. "Having finished the work, **the TV** was switched on" implies the TV finished the work ✗ → "Having finished the work, **she** switched on the TV" ✓.

## Solving Approach

1. Read the whole sentence, not just the highlighted part.
2. Name the error type (or decide there is none).
3. Eliminate options that keep the same error.
4. Among the rest, eliminate options that create a new error or change the meaning.
5. Choose the shortest clear option that remains.
6. Pick "No improvement" only after checking every error type above.

## Common Mistakes

- Choosing an option that fixes one error but introduces another.
- Avoiding "No improvement" even when the sentence is correct — it is the right answer in some questions.
- Picking the longest, most formal-sounding option.

## Placement Tips

- Subject–verb agreement, tense and parallelism account for most questions; check these first.
- Read your chosen option back into the full sentence before moving on.

## Key Takeaways

- Identify the error type, then eliminate options that keep it or add new ones.
- Watch redundancy, comparisons (like with like), parallelism and dangling modifiers.
- "No improvement" is a legitimate answer.
