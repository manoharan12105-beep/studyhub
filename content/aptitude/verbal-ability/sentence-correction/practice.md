# Sentence Correction — Practice

Choose the best replacement for the part in bold. Choose "No improvement" if the sentence is already correct.

### P1. She **don't** like coffee.

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Pattern:** Subject–verb agreement

- A) doesn't
- B) didn't not
- C) do not
- D) No improvement

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** A) doesn't

**Explanation:** "She" is singular → "does not / doesn't".

</details>

### P2. He **returned back** home late at night.

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Pattern:** Redundancy

- A) returned back to
- B) returned
- C) has returned back
- D) No improvement

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) returned

**Explanation:** "Return" already means "go back"; "back" is redundant.

</details>

### P3. There are **less** students in the class today.

**Difficulty:** Easy · **Pattern:** Fewer / less

- A) lesser
- B) few
- C) fewer
- D) No improvement

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** C) fewer

**Explanation:** "Students" are countable → "fewer". "Less" is for uncountable nouns (less water).

</details>

### P4. **Walking down the street, the trees looked beautiful.**

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Dangling modifier

- A) Walking down the street, I found the trees beautiful.
- B) Walking down the street, the trees were beautiful.
- C) The trees looked beautiful, walking down the street.
- D) No improvement

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** A) Walking down the street, I found the trees beautiful.

**Explanation:** The opening phrase must describe the subject; trees do not walk. Option A gives the phrase a proper subject ("I").

</details>

### P5. She likes reading, writing and **to paint**.

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Parallelism

- A) painting
- B) paint
- C) to painting
- D) No improvement

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** A) painting

**Explanation:** Items in a list should have the same form: reading, writing and painting.

</details>

### P6. This is the **most cleverest** student in the class.

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Double superlative

- A) more cleverer
- B) cleverest
- C) most clever than
- D) No improvement

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) cleverest

**Explanation:** Use either "-est" or "most", never both.

</details>

### P7. The population of Delhi is greater **than Mumbai**.

**Difficulty:** Medium · **Pattern:** Illogical comparison

- A) than of Mumbai
- B) than that of Mumbai
- C) then Mumbai
- D) No improvement

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** B) than that of Mumbai

**Explanation:** Compare population with population, not population with a city.

</details>

### P8. He has been working here **since five years**.

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Since / for

- A) for five years
- B) from five years
- C) before five years
- D) No improvement

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** A) for five years

**Explanation:** A period of time takes "for"; "since" needs a point of time.

</details>

### P9. Neither of the two answers **are** correct.

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Subject–verb agreement

- A) is
- B) were
- C) have been
- D) No improvement

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** A) is

**Explanation:** "Neither" is singular → "is".

</details>

### P10. **Hardly had he left than** it began to rain.

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** Fixed pairs

- A) Hardly had he left when
- B) Hardly he had left when
- C) Hardly had he left then
- D) No improvement

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** A) Hardly had he left when

**Explanation:** "Hardly … when" is the fixed pair, and "hardly" at the start requires inversion ("had he left").

</details>

### P11. The report was **well written**, so no changes were needed.

**Difficulty:** Hard · **Pattern:** No error

- A) good written
- B) well writing
- C) written good
- D) No improvement

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

**Answer:** D) No improvement

**Explanation:** "Well" (adverb) correctly modifies "written". The sentence is correct as it stands.

</details>
