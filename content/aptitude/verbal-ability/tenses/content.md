# Tenses

## Concept

A **tense** shows when an action happens (past, present, future) and how it unfolds (as a simple fact, in progress, completed, or continuing up to a point). English has three times × four aspects = **twelve tenses**.

Choosing the right tense is about reading the **signals in the sentence**: time words (*yesterday, since, by the time*), and the tense of the other clause. Most tense questions in tests can be solved by spotting one such signal.

## Rules

### The twelve tenses

| Tense | Form | Example | Use |
|-------|------|---------|-----|
| Simple present | V / V-s | She **writes**. | Habits, facts, timetables |
| Present continuous | am/is/are + V-ing | She **is writing**. | Happening now; temporary |
| Present perfect | has/have + V3 | She **has written**. | Past action with present relevance; no definite past time |
| Present perfect continuous | has/have been + V-ing | She **has been writing** for two hours. | Started in the past, still continuing |
| Simple past | V2 | She **wrote**. | Completed at a definite past time |
| Past continuous | was/were + V-ing | She **was writing**. | In progress at a past moment |
| Past perfect | had + V3 | She **had written**. | Completed before another past action |
| Past perfect continuous | had been + V-ing | She **had been writing** for an hour when… | Continued up to a past moment |
| Simple future | will + V | She **will write**. | Future action |
| Future continuous | will be + V-ing | She **will be writing**. | In progress at a future moment |
| Future perfect | will have + V3 | She **will have written** by noon. | Completed before a future moment |
| Future perfect continuous | will have been + V-ing | She **will have been writing** for an hour by noon. | Continuing up to a future moment |

(V = base verb, V2 = past form, V3 = past participle.)

### Signal words

| Signal | Usual tense |
|--------|-------------|
| every day, usually, always | Simple present |
| now, look!, at the moment | Present continuous |
| already, just, yet, ever, never (no past time given) | Present perfect |
| since + point of time, for + period (still going) | Present perfect (continuous) |
| yesterday, last week, ago, in 2015 | Simple past |
| by the time / before + past clause | Past perfect in the earlier action |
| tomorrow, next year | Future |
| by + future time | Future perfect |

### Key rules

1. **No present perfect with a definite past time.** "I **have seen** him yesterday" ✗ → "I **saw** him yesterday" ✓.
2. **Since vs for.** *Since* + a point of time (since 2015, since Monday); *for* + a period (for five years). "since five years" ✗.
3. **Stative verbs are not used in continuous forms.** *know, believe, understand, own, belong, want, like, prefer, seem*: "I **am knowing** the answer" ✗ → "I **know**" ✓.
4. **Future time clauses use the present tense.** After *when, before, after, until, as soon as, if* referring to the future: "When he **comes**, I will tell him" ✓ (not "will come").
5. **Two past actions:** the earlier one in past perfect. "The train **had left** when we reached the station" ✓.
6. **Sequence of tenses:** a past main clause is usually followed by a past form in the subordinate clause: "He said that he **was** busy." Universal truths stay present: "The teacher said that the earth **goes** round the sun."

### Conditional sentences

| Type | Form | Example | Meaning |
|------|------|---------|---------|
| Zero | If + present, present | If you heat ice, it **melts**. | General truth |
| First | If + present, will + V | If it rains tomorrow, we **will cancel** the match. | Real future possibility |
| Second | If + past, would + V | If I **were** you, I **would** accept. | Imaginary present/future |
| Third | If + past perfect, would have + V3 | If you **had told** me, I **would have helped**. | Imaginary past |

In formal English, the second conditional uses **were** for all persons: *If I were…, if he were…*

## Solving Approach

1. Find the time signal (word or other clause).
2. Decide time (past/present/future) and aspect (simple, continuous, perfect, perfect continuous).
3. Check special rules: stative verbs, future time clauses, since/for, conditionals.

## Common Mistakes

- Present perfect with *yesterday, ago, last year*.
- "Since five years" instead of "for five years".
- Continuous forms of stative verbs ("I am understanding").
- "Will" inside a future time clause ("When he will come").
- Mixing conditional forms ("If I would have known…").

## Placement Tips

- Underline time expressions first — they decide most answers.
- In two-clause sentences, decide which action happened first.
- Memorise the four conditional patterns; they appear in almost every verbal section.

## Key Takeaways

- Twelve tenses = three times × four aspects.
- Definite past time → simple past, never present perfect.
- Since + point, for + period.
- Stative verbs avoid continuous forms; future time clauses use the present.
- Earlier of two past actions → past perfect.
