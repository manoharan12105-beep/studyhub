# Error Spotting

## Concept

A sentence is split into three or four parts, labelled (A), (B), (C), with a final option (D) "No error". You must find the part that contains a grammatical or usage error. You do not have to correct it — but knowing the correction is how you confirm it.

Error spotting draws on every grammar topic. The fastest approach is a **fixed checklist** run in the same order every time, because planted errors come from a small set of rules.

## Rules

### The checklist (in order)

| # | Check | Typical planted error |
|---|-------|-----------------------|
| 1 | Subject–verb agreement | "Each of the boys **have**" |
| 2 | Tense and time words | "I **have seen** that film **last week**"; "**since** ten years" |
| 3 | Pronoun case and agreement | "between you and **I**"; "the company changed **their** policy" |
| 4 | Articles | "**an** useful", "**a** honest" |
| 5 | Prepositions | "senior **than**", "discuss **about**", "married **with**" |
| 6 | Adjective vs adverb | "speaks **good**", "drive **careful**" |
| 7 | Comparisons | "**more taller**", "**superior than**" |
| 8 | Nouns: number and countability | "**one of my friend**", "**furnitures**", "**less** students" |
| 9 | Conjunction pairs and double negatives | "**unless** … **not**", "**although** … **but**", "no sooner … **then**" |
| 10 | Word order and redundancy | "**return back**", "**hardly he had**" |

### Words that trick the ear

- **Uncountable** nouns have no plural: information, advice, furniture, luggage, equipment, news.
- **One of + plural noun**: one of my friend**s**.
- **Latin comparatives** take *to*: senior/junior/superior/inferior/prior **to**.
- **Unless** already means *if not*; do not add *not*.

### "No error"

Some sentences are correct. Choose "No error" only after running the full checklist — and expect it to be the answer in a reasonable share of questions.

## Solving Approach

1. Read the whole sentence once for meaning.
2. Run the checklist on each part.
3. Locate the error in the part where the **wrong word** sits. If the conflict is between two parts (e.g. a tense clashing with a time word), the error is in the part that must change to fix it.
4. Confirm by mentally writing the correction.

## Common Mistakes

- Marking the part that contains the subject instead of the wrong verb.
- Missing errors in short, natural-sounding phrases ("one of my friend").
- Choosing "No error" too quickly — or never choosing it.

## Placement Tips

- Subject–verb agreement and tense cover the largest share of questions; check them first.
- If a part "sounds fine" but contains a noun after "one of", "each", "every", look again.

## Key Takeaways

- Use the same checklist every time: agreement, tense, pronouns, articles, prepositions, adjective/adverb, comparisons, nouns, conjunctions, redundancy.
- The error is in the part that must change.
- "No error" is a valid answer after a full check.
