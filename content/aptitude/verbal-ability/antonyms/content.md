# Antonyms

## Concept

An **antonym** is a word opposite in meaning to another: *ancient* and *modern*. Antonym questions give a word and ask for the option **most nearly opposite** in meaning.

The most common trap is the **synonym**: question setters place a word with the same meaning among the options, and a hurried reader picks it. Always remind yourself that the task is "opposite".

## Rules

### Strategies

1. **Define the word**, then think of its opposite before reading the options.
2. **Eliminate synonyms first** — they are the main trap.
3. **Use prefixes** that create opposites: *un-* (unhappy), *in-/im-/il-/ir-* (inactive, impossible, illegal, irregular), *dis-* (dislike), *mis-* (misunderstand), *non-* (non-violent). The opposite of a prefixed word often removes the prefix.
4. **Match strength and part of speech:** the antonym of an adjective is an adjective of similar intensity.

## Solving Approach

1. Recall the meaning; predict an opposite.
2. Cross out any synonym in the options.
3. Choose the option that is opposite in meaning, matching strength and part of speech.

## Common Mistakes

- Choosing the synonym.
- Choosing a word that is merely different, not opposite (*ancient* → "small" ✗).
- Mismatching strength (*hot* ↔ *cool* is weaker than *hot* ↔ *cold*).

## Placement Tips

- Learn antonyms together with synonyms (see [Synonyms](../synonyms/content.md)); one study session covers both question types.
- When two options look opposite, pick the one that is a direct, common opposite in the same domain.

## Key Takeaways

- Predict the opposite first; eliminate synonyms.
- Prefixes (un-, in-, dis-, mis-, non-) often create or reveal antonyms.
- Match part of speech and intensity.
