# Subject-Verb Agreement

## Concept

A verb must **agree in number** with its subject: a singular subject takes a singular verb, a plural subject a plural verb.

- The boy **plays**. (singular)
- The boys **play**. (plural)

In the present tense, the singular verb usually ends in **-s** (*plays, has, is, goes*) — the opposite of nouns, where -s makes a plural. This rule is simple; the difficulty is **finding the real subject** when the sentence hides it. Subject–verb agreement is the single most common error planted in [Error Spotting](../error-spotting/content.md) and [Sentence Correction](../sentence-correction/content.md).

## Rules

### 1. Ignore the words between the subject and the verb

The verb agrees with the head subject, not with a noun inside a phrase that follows it.

- The **list** of items **is** on the desk. ✓ (not *are*)
- The **quality** of the apples **was** poor. ✓

### 2. Subjects joined by "and" are plural

- Ravi and Meena **are** friends. ✓
- **Exception:** when both nouns name one idea or one person: *Bread and butter **is** my breakfast.* *The secretary and treasurer **has** arrived* (one person holding both posts).

### 3. "Or", "either…or", "neither…nor" — agree with the nearer subject

- Neither the teacher nor the **students were** ready. ✓
- Either the students or the **teacher is** responsible. ✓

### 4. Each, every, either, neither, everyone, somebody, nobody → singular

- **Each** of the students **has** a laptop. ✓
- **Neither** of the answers **is** correct. ✓
- **Everyone** in the room **was** silent. ✓

### 5. "As well as", "along with", "together with", "in addition to" do not change the subject

- The **captain**, along with his players, **has** arrived. ✓

### 6. "A number of" is plural; "the number of" is singular

- **A number of** students **were** absent. ✓ (= many students)
- **The number of** students **is** increasing. ✓ (= the count)

### 7. Collective nouns are usually singular

- The **team is** winning. The **committee has** submitted its report. ✓
- When members act individually, a plural verb is possible, especially in British English: *The jury **were** divided in their opinions.* Placement tests mostly expect the singular.

### 8. Nouns plural in form but singular in meaning

- News, mathematics, physics, economics, measles → singular: *The news **is** good.*
- Words that are always plural: scissors, trousers, spectacles, goods → plural: *My trousers **are** torn.* But *a pair of scissors **is***.

### 9. Amounts as a single unit → singular

- Ten kilometres **is** a long distance. Five hundred rupees **is** enough. ✓

### 10. "There is / there are" agrees with the noun that follows

- There **is** a book on the table. There **are** two books on the table. ✓

### 11. "Many a" + singular noun + singular verb

- **Many a student has** failed this test. ✓

### 12. "One of" and "one of those who"

- **One** of the boys **is** absent. ✓ (subject = one)
- She is one of those **singers who have** never missed a concert. ✓ (the relative pronoun *who* refers to *singers*, so the verb is plural)

### 13. Uncountable nouns are singular

- Furniture, information, advice, luggage, equipment → *The furniture **is** old.* ✓

## Solving Approach

1. Find the verb.
2. Ask "who or what is doing this?" to find the real subject; strike out prepositional phrases and "along with"-type phrases.
3. Apply the rule for the subject type (compound, indefinite pronoun, collective, "number of" …).
4. Check that the verb's number matches.

## Common Mistakes

- Matching the verb to the nearest noun instead of the subject ("The box of pens are").
- Treating "each", "everyone" or "neither" as plural.
- Using a plural verb with "the number of".
- Using a plural verb with news, furniture, information.

## Placement Tips

- In any error-spotting question, check subject–verb agreement first.
- Mentally delete everything between the subject and the verb.
- Learn the "always singular" words: each, every, either, neither, everyone, news, information, furniture, the number of, many a.

## Key Takeaways

- Singular subject → singular verb (-s); plural subject → plural verb.
- Words between subject and verb do not count.
- And → plural; or/nor → nearer subject.
- Each/every/either/neither/everyone, the number of, many a, uncountable nouns → singular.
- A number of → plural; one of those who → plural.
