# Pronouns

## Concept

A **pronoun** stands in for a noun so we don't repeat it: "Meena lost **her** book, so **she** borrowed **mine**." Pronoun questions test three things:

1. **Case** — the correct form for the pronoun's role (subject *I* vs object *me*).
2. **Agreement** — the pronoun matches the noun it refers to (its **antecedent**) in number and gender.
3. **The right type** — *who* vs *whom*, *who* vs *which*, *its* vs *it's*, reflexive pronouns.

## Rules

### Types of pronouns

| Type | Examples |
|------|----------|
| Personal | I, you, he, she, it, we, they (subject); me, him, her, us, them (object) |
| Possessive | mine, yours, his, hers, its, ours, theirs |
| Reflexive / emphatic | myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, themselves |
| Demonstrative | this, that, these, those |
| Relative | who, whom, whose, which, that |
| Interrogative | who, whom, whose, which, what |
| Indefinite | someone, anyone, everyone, each, none, one |

### Case: subject or object?

- **Subject** form when the pronoun does the action: ***She** and **I** went home.*
- **Object** form after a verb or preposition: *between you and **me**; let you and **me** go; send it to Anita or **me**.*
- After *than* or *as*, choose the form the hidden verb needs: *She runs faster than **I** (do).* When a verb follows explicitly, the subject form is the only option: *She can run faster than **I** can.*
- **Quick test:** remove the other person. "Ravi and (I/me) went" → "I went" ✓.

### Who / whom / whose

- **who** = subject: *The boy **who** won the prize…*
- **whom** = object: *To **whom** did you give it?*
- **whose** = possession: *The girl **whose** bag was lost…*
- **Test:** if *he/she* fits, use *who*; if *him/her* fits, use *whom*. ("You gave it to him" → *to whom*.)

### Who vs which vs that

- **who** for people, **which** for things and animals, **that** for either in defining clauses.
- *The book **which/that** I bought…; the teacher **who/that** taught me…*

### Agreement with the antecedent

- Singular antecedent → singular pronoun: *The **company** changed **its** policy.* (not *their*)
- *Each of the girls has submitted **her** assignment.*
- *One* takes *one's*: *One should respect **one's** parents.*

### Reflexive pronouns

- Used when the subject and object are the same person: *She hurt **herself**.*
- Or for emphasis: *I **myself** saw it.*
- **Not** as a replacement for *I/me*: "Send it to Ravi and **myself**" ✗ → "to Ravi and **me**" ✓.

### Its vs it's

- **its** = belonging to it (*The dog wagged **its** tail*).
- **it's** = it is / it has (***It's** raining*).

### Order of pronouns

When pronouns appear together, the conventional polite order is second person, third person, first person: ***You, he and I** will go.*

## Solving Approach

1. Find the pronoun's role: subject, object, possessive.
2. Find its antecedent and check number and gender.
3. For *who/whom*, use the he/him test.
4. Remove extra people from pairs to test the case.

## Common Mistakes

- "Between you and I" (should be *me*).
- "Me and him went" (should be *He and I*).
- Using *myself* instead of *I/me*.
- *Their* for a singular collective noun like *company*.
- *It's* for possession.

## Placement Tips

- Pronoun-case errors are frequent in error spotting; check every pronoun after a preposition.
- The remove-the-other-person test settles almost every case question in seconds.

## Key Takeaways

- Subject: I, he, she, we, they, who. Object: me, him, her, us, them, whom.
- After prepositions and *let*, use object forms.
- Pronouns agree with their antecedents; singular collectives take *its*.
- Reflexives only when subject = object, or for emphasis.
