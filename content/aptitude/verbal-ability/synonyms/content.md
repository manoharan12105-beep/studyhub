# Synonyms

## Concept

A **synonym** is a word with the same or nearly the same meaning as another: *abundant* and *plentiful*. Synonym questions give a word and ask for the option **closest in meaning**.

Few words are perfect synonyms; most differ slightly in strength, tone or use. The question asks for the **closest** match — so you compare options against each other, not against a perfect definition.

Vocabulary grows fastest through roots, context and regular review; see [Vocabulary Building](../vocabulary-building/content.md) for methods. [Antonyms](../antonyms/content.md) uses the same skills in reverse.

## Rules

### Strategies

1. **Define the word in your own words first**, then look at the options.
2. **Use roots and prefixes:** *bene-* = good (benevolent = kind); *mal-* = bad; *-phile* = lover of.
3. **Recall a context:** "a *candid* interview" → honest, frank.
4. **Check tone and strength:** *annoyed* is weaker than *furious*; choose the option of similar intensity.
5. **Eliminate antonyms** — setters often include the opposite as a trap.
6. **Match the part of speech:** a synonym of an adjective is an adjective.

## Solving Approach

1. Cover the options; recall the word's meaning.
2. Look for the option that matches meaning, tone and part of speech.
3. If unsure, eliminate antonyms and unrelated words, then choose between the rest.

## Common Mistakes

- Choosing a word that is **associated** with the given word, not equal to it (*frugal* → "money" ✗).
- Falling for the antonym placed among the options.
- Ignoring strength or tone.

## Placement Tips

- Synonym questions take seconds if you know the word and are guesswork if you don't — regular reading and review matter more than tricks.
- Learn words in pairs (word + synonym + antonym) — it covers two question types at once.

## Key Takeaways

- Define first, then match meaning, tone and part of speech.
- Roots and contexts unlock unfamiliar words.
- Eliminate antonyms and merely associated words.
