# Parts of Speech

## Concept

Every word in a sentence does a job: naming something, describing it, showing an action, connecting ideas. These jobs are the **parts of speech**. Knowing them is the foundation of every other grammar topic — subject–verb agreement needs you to find the noun, tense needs the verb, and error spotting needs you to notice an adjective used where an adverb belongs.

The key idea: **a word's part of speech is decided by how it is used in the sentence, not by the word itself.** "Fast" can be four different parts of speech:

| Sentence | "fast" is a | Because it… |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| He is a **fast** runner. | Adjective | describes the noun "runner" |
| She runs **fast**. | Adverb | describes how she runs |
| They **fast** during the festival. | Verb | is the action |
| He broke his **fast** at sunset. | Noun | names a thing |

## Rules

### The eight parts of speech

| Part of speech | Job | Examples |
|----------------|-----|----------|
| Noun | Names a person, place, thing or idea | Ravi, Chennai, table, honesty |
| Pronoun | Replaces a noun | he, she, it, they, who, this |
| Verb | Shows an action or state | run, write, is, seem |
| Adjective | Describes a noun or pronoun | happy, tall, three, those |
| Adverb | Describes a verb, adjective or another adverb | quickly, very, often, here |
| Preposition | Shows the relation of a noun/pronoun to another word | in, on, at, under, after |
| Conjunction | Joins words, phrases or clauses | and, but, because, although |
| Interjection | Expresses sudden emotion | Wow!, Oh!, Alas! |

**Articles** (a, an, the) and words like *this, those, some, every* are often grouped as **determiners**; traditional grammar treats them as adjectives because they come before nouns. See [Articles](../articles/content.md).

### Tests to identify a part of speech

- **Noun:** can take *a/the* before it, or be made plural (the *decision*, *decisions*).
- **Verb:** changes with tense (*write → wrote → written*).
- **Adjective:** answers *which? what kind? how many?* about a noun.
- **Adverb:** answers *how? when? where? how much?*; many end in *-ly* (but not all: *fast, often, very*).
- **Preposition vs conjunction:** a preposition is followed by a noun/pronoun (*after the meeting*); a conjunction is followed by a clause with its own verb (*after the meeting ended*).
- **Pronoun vs adjective:** *those* alone is a pronoun (*Those are mine*); *those* before a noun is an adjective (*Those books are mine*).

### Words ending in -ing

An *-ing* word can be:

- a **verb** (part of a continuous tense): She is *swimming*.
- a **noun** (gerund): *Swimming* is good exercise.
- an **adjective** (participle): the *running* water.

## Problem Patterns

### Pattern 1: Identify the part of speech

**Approach:** find what the word is doing — naming, describing (what?), acting, connecting — then apply the tests above.

**Example:** "She sang **beautifully**." → describes how she sang → **adverb**.

### Pattern 2: Same word, different roles

**Approach:** look at the neighbouring words. A noun after the word suggests a preposition or adjective; a clause suggests a conjunction.

**Example:** "He came **after** the meeting." → preposition. "He came **after** the meeting ended." → conjunction.

### Pattern 3: Adjective vs adverb errors

**Approach:** a word describing a verb must be an adverb.

**Example:** "She speaks English **good**." ✗ → "She speaks English **well**." ✓ (*good* is an adjective; *well* is the adverb). But "I feel **good**" ✓ — after linking verbs (*feel, seem, look, be*), an adjective describes the subject.

## Common Mistakes

- Assuming every *-ly* word is an adverb (*friendly, lovely* are adjectives).
- Labelling a word by its usual role instead of its role in the sentence.
- Using adjectives to describe actions ("drive careful" ✗ → "drive carefully").
- Confusing prepositions and conjunctions with words like *after, before, since, until*.

## Placement Tips

- Direct "identify the part of speech" questions are quick marks; most errors come from not reading the whole sentence.
- In error-spotting, check every adjective/adverb pair — it is one of the most frequent planted errors.

## Key Takeaways

- Eight parts of speech: noun, pronoun, verb, adjective, adverb, preposition, conjunction, interjection.
- The role in the sentence decides the part of speech.
- Preposition + noun; conjunction + clause.
- Adverbs describe verbs; after linking verbs, use adjectives.
