# Articles

## Concept

English has three articles: **a**, **an** (indefinite) and **the** (definite). They tell the listener whether a noun is **any one** of its kind or **a specific** one both people know about.

- "I saw **a** dog" — some dog, not identified.
- "I saw **the** dog" — the particular dog we both know about.

Many nouns also take **no article** (the "zero article"): names, languages, meals, and general statements about plural or uncountable nouns. Article questions test these three choices.

## Rules

### A or an — decided by sound, not spelling

Use **an** before a vowel **sound** and **a** before a consonant **sound**.

| Word | Starts with sound | Article |
|------|-------------------|---------|
| hour, honest, heir | vowel (silent h) | **an** hour, **an** honest man |
| university, European, one-way | "y" / "w" consonant sound | **a** university, **a** European, **a** one-way street |
| MBA, MLA, FIR, X-ray | "em", "eff", "eks" (vowel sound) | **an** MBA, **an** X-ray |
| umbrella, apple | vowel | **an** umbrella |

### When to use "the"

1. Something already mentioned or known to both: *I bought a pen. **The** pen is blue.*
2. Things that are unique: ***the** sun, **the** earth, **the** sky*.
3. Superlatives and ordinals: ***the** tallest, **the** first, **the** last*; also ***the** only, **the** same*.
4. Rivers, seas, oceans, mountain ranges, groups of islands, deserts: ***the** Ganga, **the** Indian Ocean, **the** Himalayas, **the** Andamans, **the** Sahara*.
5. Musical instruments after *play*: *She plays **the** guitar.*
6. *The* + adjective for a whole group: ***the** poor, **the** rich, **the** elderly*.
7. Newspapers, famous buildings, and countries with plural or union names: ***The** Hindu, **the** Taj Mahal, **the** USA, **the** Netherlands*.

### No article

1. Most proper nouns: names of people, cities, single mountains and lakes, most countries: *Ravi, Delhi, Mount Everest, India*.
2. Languages and subjects: *She speaks French. He studies physics.*
3. Meals, in general: *I had breakfast at seven.* (But *a heavy breakfast* — an adjective brings back the article.)
4. Games and sports: *They play cricket.*
5. Plural or uncountable nouns used generally: *Dogs are loyal. Honesty is important.*
6. School, college, church, hospital, prison, bed — when used for their **main purpose**: *She goes to church on Sundays* (to pray). *She went to **the** church to meet the priest* (as a building).

### "A" before singular countable nouns meaning "one of a class"

- *He is **a** doctor.* (one of the class of doctors)
- *Once upon a time there lived **a** king.*

## Solving Approach

1. Is the noun countable and singular? Then it usually needs some article.
2. Is it specific/known/unique, or a superlative? → **the**.
3. Is it one of many, not identified? → **a/an** — choose by the first **sound**.
4. Is it a name, language, meal, game, or a general plural/uncountable? → usually **no article**.

## Common Mistakes

- Choosing a/an by the first letter instead of the first sound ("a hour", "an university").
- Omitting "the" with superlatives or rivers ("highest mountain", "Ganga flows").
- Adding "the" to languages, meals or games ("the French", "the breakfast").
- Using "the" with general plural statements ("The dogs are loyal" when meaning dogs in general).

## Placement Tips

- Say the next word aloud in your head — the sound decides a/an instantly.
- Superlative or ordinal in the sentence → look for "the".
- Article errors are common in error spotting; check every singular countable noun.

## Key Takeaways

- A/an by sound: an hour, a university, an MBA.
- The: known, unique, superlative, rivers/oceans/ranges, instruments, the + adjective groups.
- No article: names, languages, meals, games, general plurals/uncountables, institutions used for their purpose.
