# Fill in the Blanks

## Concept

A sentence has one or two blanks; you choose the word(s) that fit both **grammatically** and **logically**. The sentence always contains **clues** that point to the meaning of the missing word — your job is to find them before looking at the options.

This topic tests vocabulary in context. Grammar-only blanks (articles, prepositions, tenses) are covered in their own topics; here the focus is choosing the right **word**. For completing a sentence with a whole clause, see [Sentence Completion](../sentence-completion/content.md).

## Rules

### Clue types

| Clue | Signal words | What it tells you |
|------|--------------|-------------------|
| Contrast | but, although, despite, however, yet, while | The blank is **opposite** to the other idea |
| Cause / result | because, since, so, therefore, as a result | The blank **follows logically** from the other idea |
| Support / restatement | and, also, in other words, that is, ":" | The blank **matches** the other idea |
| Degree | so … that, too … to | The blank is **strong enough** to cause the result |

**Example (contrast):** "Despite his wealth, he lived a ___ life." *Despite* signals contrast with wealth → **simple**.

**Example (cause):** "He was so ___ that he fell asleep during the lecture." The result (falling asleep) → **tired**.

### Double blanks

- Solve the **easier** blank first and eliminate options.
- The two words must fit **together** — check the relationship between the blanks (often both positive, both negative, or one causing the other).

### Grammar check

The chosen word must fit the grammar: noun vs adjective vs verb form, singular vs plural, and collocation (*make* a decision, *pay* attention, *attention* **to** detail).

## Solving Approach

1. Read the whole sentence and cover the options.
2. Find the clue word and decide: contrast, cause, support or degree.
3. Predict the meaning (or a simple word) for the blank.
4. Match your prediction to the options; check grammar.
5. For double blanks, test both words in the sentence together.

## Common Mistakes

- Choosing a word that "sounds nice" without checking the clue.
- Missing a contrast word such as *despite* or *although*.
- In double blanks, choosing an option where only one word fits.

## Placement Tips

- Predicting the word before reading options prevents being misled by attractive distractors.
- Words with strong negative or positive tone are easy to sort; decide the tone the blank needs first.

## Key Takeaways

- Every sentence has a clue: contrast, cause, support or degree.
- Predict, then match; check grammar and collocation.
- Double blanks must work together.
