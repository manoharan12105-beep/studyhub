# Direct and Indirect Speech

## Concept

**Direct speech** quotes the exact words: *He said, "I am busy."*
**Indirect (reported) speech** reports them without quotation marks: *He said that he was busy.*

When reporting, three things usually change, because the report is made **later** and by **someone else**:

1. **Tense** moves one step back (when the reporting verb is past).
2. **Pronouns** change to fit the reporter's point of view.
3. **Time and place words** change (*today → that day, here → there*).

The sentence type (statement, question, command, exclamation) also decides the reporting verb and connector.

## Rules

### 1. Tense changes (reporting verb in the past)

| Direct | Indirect |
|--------|----------|
| Simple present (am/is, write) | Simple past (was, wrote) |
| Present continuous (is writing) | Past continuous (was writing) |
| Present perfect (has written) | Past perfect (had written) |
| Simple past (wrote) | Past perfect (had written) |
| will / shall / can / may | would / should / could / might |
| must (obligation) | had to (or must) |

**No change when:**

- the reporting verb is in the present or future: *He **says**, "I am tired."* → *He says that he **is** tired.*
- the reported words are a universal truth or habitual fact: *The teacher said, "The earth goes round the sun."* → *…that the earth **goes** round the sun.*

### 2. Pronoun changes

Change pronouns to match who is now speaking and who is being spoken to.
*She said to me, "I will help you."* → *She told me that **she** would help **me**.*

### 3. Time and place words

| Direct | Indirect |
|--------|----------|
| now | then |
| today / tonight | that day / that night |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| yesterday | the previous day / the day before |
| next week | the following week |
| last week | the previous week |
| ago | before |
| here | there |
| this / these | that / those |

### 4. Sentence types

| Type | Reporting verb | Connector | Example |
|------|----------------|-----------|---------|
| Statement | said / told + object | that | He said that he was busy. |
| Yes/no question | asked | if / whether | She asked him **if he was** coming. |
| Wh- question | asked | the wh-word | He asked me **where I lived**. |
| Command | told / ordered | to + verb (not to + verb) | The officer ordered the soldiers **to stand** still. |
| Request | requested | to + verb | She requested me **to help** her. |
| Exclamation | exclaimed with joy/sorrow | that | She exclaimed with joy that it was a beautiful day. |
| Suggestion ("Let's") | suggested | that … should | He suggested that they **should go** for a walk. |

In reported questions, use **statement word order** (subject before verb) and no question mark: "where I lived", not "where did I live".

*Said to* becomes **told** (statements) or **asked** (questions): *He said to me, "…"* → *He told me that…*

## Solving Approach

1. Identify the sentence type → choose the reporting verb and connector.
2. Check the reporting verb's tense → backshift if past (unless a universal truth).
3. Change pronouns from the reporter's viewpoint.
4. Change time and place words.
5. For questions, restore statement word order.

## Common Mistakes

- Keeping question word order in reported questions ("He asked where did I live").
- Forgetting to change *tomorrow*, *yesterday*, *here*.
- Backshifting universal truths.
- Using "said to" in indirect speech instead of "told" or "asked".
- Reporting commands with "that" instead of "to".

## Placement Tips

- Eliminate options with the wrong reporting verb first (asked for questions, ordered/told for commands).
- Then check tense and time words; usually only one option gets both right.

## Key Takeaways

- Past reporting verb → tense moves one step back; present reporting verb → no change.
- Universal truths keep their tense.
- Questions: asked + if/whether or wh-word + statement order.
- Commands/requests: told/ordered/requested + (not) to + verb.
